Byte LUT
byte 0 … 255: 256 colours spread as far apart as the RGB cube allows (257 points placed to maximise Σ ⁴√(distance to the nearest other colour), the eight corners pinned, white left out so every byte shows against the page; the closest two colours are 44 apart on the 0–255 scale). Which byte gets which colour is tuned to the dictionaries: the frequent bytes of each one (Latin letters, the UTF-8 bytes of Han, Armenian, Cyrillic, Georgian, Arabic, Devanagari, Hangul and kana text) are kept as far apart in colour as the palette allows. Every pixel holds a 128-bit string on a plane with edges (no wrap-around). Each turn runs the four swap phases in the fixed order UD0, LR0, UD1, LR1; the edge column or row that a phase's brick pattern leaves out pairs along the edge instead, with a brick offset that alternates by turn, so every pixel takes part in four exchanges a turn (a corner in three). Then comes a block of bit-plane median filtering passes (entropy decrease), then the local changer: every pixel's 16 bytes are replaced by what the changer makes of them, so the decompressed text or the Brainfuck tape becomes the pixel's new content. A Decoder changer reads the 16 bytes as a coded stream instead, Deflate, LZ4, Snappy, a Brotli-like coder, a prefix code, Morse, Rice-coded residuals, WebP's boolean decoder, PNG filters or a JPEG block, and writes what it decodes back over the pixel. With Cells set to pairs, the transforms and Brainfuck see the two cells of a column pair joined into one 32-byte cell, the roles alternating by turn; a CPU changer or the LZ decompressor instead gives every pair of neighbouring pixels (left–right and up–down) two persistent machines of its own, one per direction, each of which runs one instruction (one copy command for the decompressor) per phase on the two pixels joined as the Cells box says, its own pixel in the first role, as its 32-byte memory; a turn makes 2 such rounds of the four phases by default (the CPU cycles per frame box) before the lattice is drawn, and the machines keep their registers from frame to frame (the never box), unless the CPU reset box starts them fresh, PC 0 and registers 0, every n frames. Last, mutation flips a random share of all bits (off by default, 0 %; 0.001 % would be about 335 bits a turn on a 512 × 512 grid). A swap phase pairs every pixel with one neighbour, left–right (LR) or up–down (UD), at an even (0) or odd (1) offset, so within one turn every pixel exchanges once with each of its four neighbours. For each pair the two strings are compared position by position; where the bits differ, the 32 bits around that position in each pixel are taken, the two contexts packed without regard to order and hashed once, so the decision does not depend on which pixel is which. Every position whose hash falls below the threshold is exchanged, a uniform crossover; swaps conserve the total number of set bits. The slider value p runs along the x axis from the left-edge value to the right-edge value, each 0 to 1, with the column's position taken to [−1, 1], its magnitude raised to the Gradient power (0.07 by default, so the gradient is steep at the centre and nearly flat towards the edges) and the sign put back; the crossover rate is 2 p (1 − p), so p = 0 and p = 1 are the identity and p = 0.5 the maximal mixing. A median filtering pass works on each of the 128 bit planes separately: it looks at every bit that disagrees with the majority of its neighbourhood (the 3 × 3 block by default, or itself and its four 4-connected neighbours, or the 5 × 5 block), hashes the pixel's 32 bits around that position, and if the hash is below the smoothing threshold the bit takes the majority value. The median filtering probability is a linear gradient along the y axis from the top-edge value to the bottom-edge value. The image is drawn through a render buffer: each pixel's colour is an exponential average of the rendered frames whose half-time is 200 ms or 4 turns, whichever is longer at the current turn rate, and a second buffer integrates the output of the first in the same way (two stages in cascade), so flicker is damped and the slower dynamics carry more weight. With a temporal sharpening s other than 0 one more buffer integrates the last stage's output (the frame itself when averaging is off) at twice the half-time and the frame shown is (1 + s) × the last stage − s × the slow one (s = 1 by default: the average plus what the slow stage has not yet followed; 0 the average alone, −1 the slow stage itself, 5 a strong sharpening). The accumulators are f32x4 vectors, one per pixel and stage. The averaged image can then be sharpened spatially: each channel becomes the pixel plus p × (the pixel − the mean of its four 4-connected neighbours), clamped to 0–255, with p from the Sharpening slider (0 by default, off; 1 the classic 2 × pixel − mean; −1 the mean itself, a blur). Neither step feeds back into the lattice. Each pixel's bits are also read as a stream of copy commands against a static dictionary (by default the Declaration of Independence; the Dictionary box offers short public-domain texts in many scripts, each as UTF-8 bytes), and expanded to exactly 128 bits. The first ⌈log₂ V⌉ bits (the "LZ cursor read" range V, the whole dictionary by default: 13 bits for the Declaration's 8,122 bytes) select a start byte, the "LZ cursor offset" is added, and the cursor wraps at the end of the text. Every command is a prefix-coded copy length of 2 to N bytes followed by a 3-bit skip: the cursor skips that many bytes, the copy is taken from the dictionary at the cursor, and the cursor moves past it. Copies are the only way to make output; there are no literals and no distances, and neither the image nor the output is ever read back. The decompression slider sets N, the longest copy. Optionally two neighbouring cells (columns 2i and 2i + 1) are changed together: their 256 bits are joined, either concatenated (A then B on even turns, B then A on odd turns) or bit-interleaved (A on the even bit positions and B on the odd ones, swapped on odd turns), the changer runs once on the 32-byte joint cell (a CPU then has 32 bytes of memory, the decompressor 256 input bits and 32 output bytes), and the result is split back by the same mapping in reverse. The changers defined on exactly 128 bits (PDP-8, UNIVAC 1101, CDC 6600) see the joint cell as two consecutive 16-byte cells; the DCT becomes a 32-point transform over it. The local changer can instead be a Brainfuck interpreter: the 16 bytes are a program (values 0–7 are the eight commands, anything else a comment) run on a 16-cell tape, and the tape afterwards is the new content; the "Random bytes 0–7" start fills the field with programs. Simpler processors are available too: do nothing, invert, reverse bytes, the extremes rule, 17 to the byte after it, 5 to the byte before it), a DCT and its inverse (16-point on a cell, 32-point on a pair). And sixteen machines, a Zilog Z80, MOS 6502, Intel 8080, Subleq, CHIP-8, Intel 4004, Game Boy SM83, Intel 8051, Motorola 6809, RISC-V RV32EC, DEC PDP-8 (8 words of 12 bits), DEC PDP-11 and Motorola 68000: the 16 bytes are the whole memory, the selected number of instructions run, the memory is written back, and halting instructions are NOPs. Threads splits the grid into horizontal bands, one Web Worker with its own wasm instance per band; each band receives a few halo rows from its neighbours once per turn and runs the whole turn locally, bit-identical to the single-threaded run. Scale 1:1 maps one lattice pixel to one device pixel and blits the wasm pixel buffer straight to the canvas. Click a pixel to inspect it; shift-drag paints pixels to all ones, ctrl-shift-drag to all zeros. Keys: space play/pause, n step, r random.
