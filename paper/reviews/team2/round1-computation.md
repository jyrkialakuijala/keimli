## Findings

**1. Provenance is in, and it works, with four gaps.** Every output in the new folders carries a stamp with script, script hash, wasm hash, harness hash, commit and dirty flag. The wasm hash `90ec8c30bca92b5b` is the same in all 2,000-odd files and equals the current `build/keimli.wasm`, which was rebuilt today (05:39) from the committed sources: the build is deterministic. The script hashes stamped on `closure-vs-survival`, `replicators-summary2`, `map-exact`, `replicase` (later version) and `bff` (census version) equal the committed files. Gaps: (a) the harness hash `419b7f684c76f4ef` stamped on every closure, droplet, heredity and frame-mechanism run and on 970 of the 1,150 replicase runs matches no commit (48f9653's harness hashes to `dd5ac3a4…`, HEAD's to `d4ef603b…`); the only committed change is the budget line, which those scripts do not use, but the version that made the mechanism data cannot be recovered. (b) The `dirty` flag ignores untracked files, and every experiment script was untracked on 10-21, so "48f9653+dirty" says nothing; the script hash is what carries the information. (c) `entropy-curves.mjs` output has no provenance field, against "every new output carries it". (d) The 780 per-seed lead files are bare arrays with no stamp; only the aggregate file has one. Also: `tools/ca-harness.mjs:17` still defaults `altOrder = false`; every new script passes `'rotate'` explicitly (I grepped all thirteen), so the data are right, but the next script that forgets gets the fixed order silently.

**2. The size dependence of takeover is a threshold artefact; the qualitative conclusion is stronger than stated.** From the stored lead curves (20, 20 and 10 seeds; 10-turn resolution), the median turn at which Brotli 12's fixed point reaches a share on the frame:

| share | 128² | 256² | 512² |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 % | 60 | 70 | 60 |
| 25 % | 110 | 120 | 110 |
| 40 % | 250 | 255 | 260 |
| 50 % | 770 | 920 | 1050 |

The final share is 54–57 %, so the 50 % mark sits on the plateau, where the crossing time is set by the plateau's height and the creep, not by growth. At 25 % and 40 % the times are size-independent within 5 %. For Brotli 13 (plateau 63–67 %): 25 % at 185/195/200, 40 % at 275/340/355, 50 % at 380/510/590. `replicators-v2.md` item 2 ("grows 1.35–1.6× for a fourfold side") should be replaced by "size-independent at every share below the plateau", which is the nucleation signature without qualification. The first panel's point that t₅₀ was taken at the saturation tail still applies to the corrected pipeline. Report the share curves, or the time to a fixed fraction of the plateau.

**3. The closure index: the correlation is mostly theorems; the empirical content is five points, and I add a sixth.** `closure-vs-survival.md` reproduces (Pearson 0.786; Spearman 0.77). But of the 15 types, five are fixed points, whose "random-phase domain" is a uniform configuration and survives by Lemma 1, and five are Gray cycles, closed by the DEFINITION.md §4 proposition. Those ten points are at (1, 1) by proof. The five generic cycles are the test: three open types (closure 6–39 %) die; the two 5-to-12 cycles at 95 % also die under partial settling. Over those five, r = 0.57. My Swarm F run adds a fourth open type: its 3-cycle (closure 33–52 %) in random phases at swap 0.1 holds 0 / 0 / 0 / 100 % at median 0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1, exactly like Brotli 12's. So the honest statement is a count, not a correlation: closed types hold (10 of 10, two of them by proof), open types melt (4 of 4), and 95 % is not closed enough (0 of 2). Closure is necessary, and the index does not say how much is sufficient. The "r = 0.79 over 15 types" should go from the paper; the 2 × 2 count with the two exceptions should replace it. The experiment that would make the index quantitative is more generic cycles from the other searched dictionaries (Brotli 1's 2-cycle with a 78 % basin, the Gray 32 design's strays), which `closure.mjs` and `droplets.mjs` can do in minutes.

**4. DEFINITION.md §4 is correct as far as I can check it, with two stated dependencies.** Lemma 1 is checked on the module. Lemma 3 is the prefix-free-code identity for a stopping time and is right; it bounds entropy, not image size, as the text now says. The pointer lemma depends on two implementation facts (copies past an empty output read the dictionary window; the two copy commands fill 16 bytes and stop) that are verified bit-exactly on the 24 states of the three-species design and, indirectly, by the 100 % closure of all five Gray types. The proposition's proof is sound: any bitwise combination of states of one type keeps the fixed prefix and some 3-bit pattern in header bits 4–6, every pattern is a Gray value, and the tail is never read. Two limits the text should state: the proposition covers one type's own states only, so "mutually closed" Gray types (`mechanism.md` §3, C(#1, #2) = 100 %) is empirical, since crossovers of two types mix the bits of H₀ as well; and Lemma 1 is stated for single-cell rewriting, which is all the experiments use.

**5. The Declaration is still unmeasured, against the response table.** RESPONSE.md row 4 says the detector goes "up to 64 by default (any length via kmax)" and is done. Every Declaration file in `replicators-v2/` has `kmax: 64` (`replicators-v2.sh`, `-v2b.sh`, `-v2c.sh` all pass `kmax=64`), and the Declaration's attractors have periods 154 and 182 (`ablation-main.md` §1). Its rows read "leader k 1, basin 0.00 %, final 0 %" because the detector cannot see its cycles. `replicators-v2.md` item 5, "the Declaration and the Z80 produce no takeover in any setting", is therefore not a measurement for the Declaration. The same was my round-2 finding for the first batch. Rerunning four settings × 10 seeds at kmax = 200 is about 7 CPU-hours (the ring is 401 states per cell, 105 MB at 256²).

## The seven questions

**Q1. Settled, partly, not at all.** Settled: provenance (with the gaps in finding 1), the rotating order in every script, censoring-aware medians with intervals, connected correlations and the finite-size series for the medium, the exact fixed-point count, the snapshot bug, the formal definition and Lemma 1. Partly: the period detector (raised to 64, which leaves the one dictionary that needed it unmeasured, finding 5); the mechanism (a necessary condition with a proof for the designed types, not a predictor, finding 3); takeover statistics (uncensored now, but still read at the plateau, finding 2); the processor arm (finding 6 and Q4). Not at all: one turn engine and a parity test for the page; the archived build; the docs header; the harness default; salt-by-position as a control in the v2 pipeline (physics' first-panel runs stand alone); first appearance at 32²–64², where my round-2 data showed the lattice creating basin entries.

**Q2.** "The medium, not the basin, picks the winner" is now explained in this sense: uniform fixed-point domains are fixed points of the lattice map (proof), designed cycles are closed under every bitwise combination of their states (proof), generic cycles are open and melt under mixing with partial settling (4 of 4 measured, including the page's default), and on the frame the closed type nucleates in the melt and fills the mixing band while the open type keeps the edge columns and the fully settled rows (`frame-mechanism`, three seeds and a second dictionary). The closure index is the right one-operator quantity for the necessary condition; it is not sufficient (the two 95 % cases), and the correlation should not be reported as its evidence. The proof in §4 is correct (finding 4).

**Q3.** Complete in the v2 results; the index and the drafts still contain all of them (finding 10). Nothing new rests on them.

**Q4.** Three results and one definition. Results: the BFF census (12 of 12 evolved copiers send (R | X) to the fixed point (R | mirror R) in one step for 256 of 256 partners; non-instruction bytes 100 % inherited), the Redcode imp on joined pairs (a fixed point of the cell map; 77 % mean takeover at 256 instructions over 5 seeds; Kaplan–Meier median 144 on the frame), and the dose-response separation (processors keep thousands of attractor types and lower block entropy with the budget; the decoder collapses to 1–3 types), which is seed 1 on single cells. The definition: in the replicase ablation, "verbatim" is defined as copying the unread tape bits, and heredity is measured as those bits surviving in descendants, so 1.00 against 0.00 is close to a tautology; the ablation's real content is the other switches (self-reference, protection, the novelty supply, the error threshold at swap ≈ 0.01). "Replicators of computational life are fixed points and short cycles" is a result for BFF and Core War's imp, and a conjecture for the rest; the 6502's undecoded pair leader (61–69 % at 16 instructions, seeds 1–3) is the one processor leader not yet shown to be a fixed point. "Heredity needs verbatim copying of unread tape" is a BFF result and an ablation definition. As a second paper it is a short one, and it needs seeds.

**Q5.** The thesis is now what the first panel asked for: the decoder as a CA rule with an exact census, designed attractors with proofs, a medium that selects by closure, and processors as a contrast measured with the same instruments. That is a map paper for *Artificial Life*, with the processor arm as its last section or a separate short paper. What changed since the first panel: the dichotomy has an operational form (whole-cell collapse against local order with the information kept, at matched collision entropy) instead of a slogan, and the mechanism has a theorem in it.
